# Preserve the boundary: five academic rewrite decisions

A free workbook from the PaperHumanizer product team for postgraduate researchers and writers using English as an additional language. Prepared with AI assistance, October 31, 2026. Every passage below is a fictional language-editing exercise, not a research finding or measured product result.

A rewrite can keep the topic but change the claim. Negation, quantifiers, time limits, and exclusions often carry information that a smoother sentence still needs. Compare the original and candidate in context before approving an edit.

## 1. Not assessed is not a negative result

- Original: “The review did not assess long-term retention.”
- Candidate: “The review found no long-term retention.”
- Decision: reject. The candidate invents a negative finding for an outcome the original says was unassessed.
- Language-only alternative for this exercise: “Long-term retention was not assessed in the review.”

## 2. Not every is not none

- Original: “Not every response contained an example.”
- Candidate: “No response contained an example.”
- Decision: reject. The original rules out universal coverage; the candidate rules out any examples at all. Do not infer a count of responses from either sentence beyond what the text states.
- Language-only alternative: “An example was not present in every response.” Check whether the surrounding manuscript makes the intended quantifier sufficiently clear before using this alternative.

## 3. Only one channel is not all channels

- Original: “Only written comments were coded; spoken feedback was not analysed.”
- Candidate: “All feedback was coded.”
- Decision: reject. The candidate erases the excluded channel and broadens the scope of the analysis.
- Language-only alternative: “Coding was limited to written comments; spoken feedback was not analysed.”

## 4. A choice is not an observed action

- Original: “Participants could decline the optional interview.”
- Candidate: “Participants declined the optional interview.”
- Decision: reject. The candidate turns a permission or option into a claim about what participants actually did.
- Language-only alternative: “Participants had the option to decline the interview.” Check that the surrounding text still makes its optional status clear.

## 5. A local observation is not a general conclusion

- Original: “These observations concern one course during the pilot week.”
- Candidate: “These observations apply to university courses.”
- Decision: reject. The candidate drops both the setting and the time restriction and makes an unsupported generalisation.
- Language-only alternative: “These observations are limited to one course during the pilot week.”

These alternatives are examples of editing the invented source sentences, not certified rewrites of a real manuscript. Actual revisions must be checked against the methods, sources, tables, and surrounding argument.

## A private review record

Copy this block into your approved private workspace:

```text
Original locator and version:
Candidate locator and version:
What was assessed or done:
What was explicitly excluded:
Who / where / when:
Negation, quantifier, condition, or uncertainty to preserve:
Accepted / rejected / unresolved:
Reason and source checked:
Reviewer and next action:
```

## Use review prompts, not automatic replacement rules

Search terms such as `not`, `no`, `only`, `every`, and `could` can help locate passages to inspect. Their presence or absence is not itself an error. The same word can serve a different function in a quotation, another study, or a different part of the argument. Read the full passage and ask a co-author about unresolved meaning. No keyword checker can certify the scientific claim.

Follow your institution's, collaborators', and target journal's actual AI-use and disclosure policies. This resource does not prescribe one universal disclosure statement.
