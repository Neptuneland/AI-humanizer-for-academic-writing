# A response-to-reviewers worksheet for academic writers

A copyable English scaffold for researchers preparing a revision letter, especially when English is an additional language. Use your editor's instructions and your field's conventions first. This worksheet is free to read and copy; no product account is needed.

Prepared by the PaperHumanizer product team with AI assistance, October 23, 2026. It is a writing resource, not a journal-endorsed template. All examples below are fictional and are not reports of completed research or manuscript changes.

## 1. Build a change log before polishing the English

Keep each reviewer comment beside the action it requires. Split a multi-part comment into separate rows. If your review is confidential, keep the completed worksheet private; do not paste reviews, unpublished results, or identifying details into a public issue or repository.

| Comment ID | Exact comment or clearly marked summary | Action and reason | Status | Location in revised manuscript |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1.1 | [Reviewer comment] | [Clarify / add evidence / analyze / explain why not] | [Planned / completed / not undertaken] | [Section, page, lines, manuscript version] |
| R1.2 | [Next part of comment] | [Specific action] | [Status] | [Location] |

Do not use “we have added” for a planned change. Resolve the action first, then check every location against the final submitted file. Page and line numbers can move when the manuscript is exported.

## 2. Copy this response block for each comment

**Reviewer [number], comment [number]**

> [Exact comment, or a clearly labeled faithful summary if the editor permits it.]

**Response:** Thank you for highlighting [specific issue]. [Direct answer to the question or concern.] We [describe the completed action and why it addresses the issue].

**Change in the revised manuscript:** [Section, page, lines; specify the manuscript version used for these locations.]

**Revised text:** “[Quote the actual revised sentence, where useful.]”

If no change was made, replace the last two fields with **Reason no change was made:** [Evidence-based explanation of the constraint and any alternative clarification actually added.]

Never invent an experiment, citation, analysis, approval, or manuscript change to complete a sentence frame.

## 3. A small language bank

Adapt these frames to the facts. Politeness should not hide an unanswered question.

| Situation | English frame to adapt |
|---|---|
| An explanation was unclear | “We agree that the original wording did not clearly distinguish [A] from [B]. We have revised [location] to make this distinction explicit.” |
| A method needs detail | “We have added [specific methodological detail] in [location], so readers can understand [specific part of the procedure].” |
| A claim was too strong | “We have replaced [original wording] with [revised wording] to reflect the limits of [study design or evidence].” |
| A requested analysis was completed | “We conducted [actual analysis] using [actual method]. The result was [actual result], reported in [location].” |
| A request cannot be completed | “We understand the value of [request]. We cannot complete it with the available [data/resources] because [specific reason]. We have clarified this limitation in [location].” |
| A disagreement needs explanation | “We appreciate this interpretation. Our reading differs because [specific evidence or design feature]. We have clarified [actual change] to make the basis of our interpretation clearer.” |
| A reference was suggested | “We reviewed the suggested work and [explain its relevance or why it does not support the specific claim]. [Describe any reference or wording change actually made.]” |
| The same concern appears elsewhere | “This concern also appears in comment [ID]. We address it here in relation to [this comment's specific point]: [brief direct answer].” |

Avoid “the reviewer misunderstood” when explaining a disagreement. Explain the evidence and the revised wording. Do not cite a paper you have not checked just to make the reply sound agreeable.

## 4. Worked fictional example: a narrower claim

**Comment:** “The discussion describes this association as causal. Please qualify that statement.”

**Weak reply:** “Thank you. We have improved the language throughout.”

This leaves the reviewer unable to locate the answer or assess what changed.

**More specific reply:** “Thank you for highlighting this overstatement. In this fictional example, the study is observational. We have replaced ‘caused higher attendance’ with ‘was associated with higher attendance’ in the Discussion. We have also stated that unmeasured confounding may explain the association.”

**Location:** “[Insert the real page and line numbers only after making and checking the change.]”

The revision narrows the claim. It does not prove that the association is valid or that the response will satisfy the reviewer.

## 5. Final author check

- Every comment has a direct answer, including sub-parts.
- Completed changes match the manuscript; unavailable work is described honestly.
- Numbers, terminology, citations, and the direction and strength of claims agree across the letter and paper.
- Quoted revised text is copied from the final manuscript, not an earlier draft.
- Page/line references match the version the editor will receive.
- Coauthors have reviewed substantive claims and any disagreements.
- Journal and institutional rules for AI assistance and disclosure have been followed.
