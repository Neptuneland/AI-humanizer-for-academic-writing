# Citation checks after an academic rewrite

A free review worksheet from the PaperHumanizer product team for students, postgraduate researchers and writers using English as an additional language. Prepared with AI assistance, November 2, 2026. Examples below are fictional teaching material, not real references or measured product output.

## A citation can survive while its meaning changes

Keeping an author name and year does not establish that the revised sentence is still supported. Compare the proposition, attribution, scope and citation placement with the source you actually read.

Fictional example:

- Original: “Study A examined written feedback in one seminar.”
- Candidate: “Written feedback improves learning across university courses (Study A).”
- Decision: reject. The candidate adds an improvement claim and broadens the setting. An unchanged citation marker cannot supply evidence for those additions.
- Language-only candidate: “Study A investigated written feedback within a single seminar.” Check the actual source before using any real equivalent.

“Study A” is a placeholder, not a bibliographic entry. Do not add it to a manuscript.

## Four separate checks

1. **Identity:** Does the marker resolve to the intended bibliography entry? Check names, year, version and identifiers against the actual source, using your institution's access route.
2. **Support:** What exact passage, table or figure supports this proposition? Record a locator. A matching title or abstract alone may be insufficient.
3. **Attribution:** Is this your finding, a cited author's finding, or your interpretation? Preserve that distinction after combining sentences.
4. **Reach:** Does the source concern the same outcome, population, setting and uncertainty? Keep limitations visible.

Do not silently substitute a related-looking reference when you cannot retrieve the original. Mark the item unresolved and ask the author or co-author.

## A two-sentence exercise

Fictional original: “Study A examined written comments. Our pilot also collected spoken feedback.”

Fictional candidate: “Study A examined written comments and spoken feedback.”

Reject: the candidate transfers the pilot's action to another source. Splitting or merging sentences can change attribution even without changing individual nouns.

Review each proposition separately. The correct repair depends on the actual evidence and intended attribution, not a universal rule to place a citation at the end of every sentence.

## Finish in the original document

Use the citation manager or document editor to check fields, cross-references and bibliography after importing revised prose. Review the exported file as well: an editor preview does not prove every field survived export. Follow the target journal's citation style and actual AI-use policy.

This worksheet is a manual review aid. It does not verify sources automatically, certify accuracy or prescribe one universal disclosure statement.
