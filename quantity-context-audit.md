# Numbers need their context: an academic revision audit

A free manual worksheet from the PaperHumanizer product team for postgraduate researchers and writers using English as an additional language. Prepared with AI assistance, November 3, 2026. Every number and passage here is invented teaching material, not a research finding or measured product output.

## 1. Equal percentages can refer to different groups

Fictional original: “Of 40 invited participants, 30 responded. Twelve of the respondents selected option A.”

Candidate: “Forty percent of invited participants selected option A.”

Reject. Twelve out of 30 respondents is 40%; twelve out of 40 invited participants is 30%. The candidate changes the denominator. A language edit must preserve which group the number describes. Do not infer the nonrespondents' choices.

A defensible language-only candidate for this exercise is: “Twelve of the 30 respondents selected option A; 40 participants had been invited.” Check the actual methods and table before editing a real paper.

## 2. Keep the unit attached to the value

Fictional original: “The interval was 250 ms.”

Candidate: “The interval was 250 seconds.”

Reject. The digits survive but the unit changes. If you intentionally convert units, record and independently check the conversion; do not allow a prose revision to make it silently. Symbols may also be damaged by copy and paste or export.

Review the quantity, unit, scale and the thing measured as one item. Identical digits alone do not establish equivalence.

## 3. Percentage points are not relative percent change

Fictional original: “The share increased from 20% to 25%.”

Candidate: “The share increased by 5%.”

Mark unresolved and clarify the intended description. The difference is five percentage points; the relative increase from the starting share is 25%. State the baseline and calculation when the distinction matters.

Do not add a causal explanation merely because one number is larger. This exercise establishes arithmetic, not why a result changed.

## 4. Preserve an interval's role

Fictional original: “Values ranged from 8 to 12.”

Candidate: “The mean was 10.”

Reject. A range does not establish a mean. A midpoint can be calculated from its endpoints, but it is a different quantity. Do not invent a summary statistic because it makes a sentence shorter.

The same review discipline applies to labels such as median, mean, standard deviation and confidence interval: verify the intended statistic against the actual source. This worksheet is not a method for estimating missing statistics.

## Review procedure

1. Read the complete source sentence and its surrounding argument.
2. Locate the corresponding table, figure or method.
3. Compare each quantity as a value-context pair, including exclusions and missing observations.
4. Ask the author about unresolved differences. Do not silently “correct” a source value.
5. Inspect the final document export for broken symbols, superscripts and decimal separators.

A literal number search can locate differences, but cannot decide which group, unit or statistic the author intended. Matching numbers do not prove matching meaning. Follow your actual institutional and journal AI-use policies.
